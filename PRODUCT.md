# Product

**User.** High-agency people (founders, engineers, operators, clinicians, policy staff, researchers) who want to do
something consequential and default to whatever the news cycle is watching.

**Painful job.** "Given what I can actually do, where does one unit of my effort matter most?" 80,000 Hours gives career
advice, GiveWell ranks charities for money only, petition sites ask for a click. None routes a skill to a neglected cause
with a dated test.

**Promise.** In two minutes: see which preventable deaths the world looks away from, then get one assignment shaped to
your capabilities, with a resolution condition you write and a date it resolves.

**Wedge.** The gap itself is shareable: a household-air-pollution death draws millions of times less Wikipedia
attention than a shark-attack death. Every cause page answers searches like "how many people die from X each year" with
a sourced figure and carries its own share card.

**Why now.** Attention is visibly skewed by feeds; the data to show it (WHO estimates, Wikimedia pageviews) is public and
refreshes itself.

**Activation metric.** A visitor gets an assignment and acts on it: copies the commitment, downloads the calendar date or
posts it publicly. Public commitments are countable today with no key (GitHub issues labelled `commitment`); the
in-page events (`assignment`, `commit-copy`, `commit-ics`, `commit-public`, `share`) are counted once GoatCounter is on.

**First revenue event.** None on GitHub Pages (non-commercial). The marked slot is a support link (`SUPPORT_URL`). Later,
off Pages: placement fees from implementing organisations for routed specialists, or a grant.

**Won't do in v1.** Accounts, stored declarations, feeds, likes, leaderboards, donations, multiple assignments, emails to
organisations, any claim that an organisation endorses the routing.
